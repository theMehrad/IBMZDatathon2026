L0Logs to tables

Logon, USB, file and web logs (about 15 GB for r4.2, 31 GB for r5.2) become three Spark tables per person per day.

r4.2 alone has 1,003 computers and 475 websites. The web log moved from a stretch goal to core, because rare-site visits turned out to be a strong signal.

L4Early warning

Replay the logs one day at a time, using only the past. The 20 highest running scores form that day's review list.

Insiders on the list before their last malicious action

Grey is the 28-day baseline on the same 20-name list. Under our running rule it gets only 33 and 28, and a random score gets 10 and 11. On average, about half of each insider's labelled malicious actions (54%, 48%) came on later days. Median delay: 0 days for leakers (most were already listed) and 1.5 to 3 weeks for job-hunters and snoopers. Analyst load is about 5 new names a week.

Access requests drop from 2.31 to 1.77 per person-month (3.15 to 2.51 on r5.2); retrained monthly, 31% fewer than own history on both. It suggests rather than decides: only 31% and 44% of its extra grants were later used. Removal suggestions: 6.0% and 3.6% of the sites people hold go unused afterwards. The new-to-company rule blocks 100% of wikileaks and keylogger visits, costing 5 normal people 8 days in total on r4.2.
